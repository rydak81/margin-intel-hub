# Audience and creator-pattern research

Reviewed November 7, 2026. Qualitative study of public positioning and formats, not a causal study of recommendation algorithms. No private analytics, follower-growth dataset or viral-success prediction was available.

## What the sampled publishers visibly do

| Example | Observable positioning | Useful design principle for Ryan |
|---|---|---|
| Ethan Mollick / One Useful Thing | Research-based interpretation of AI's implications for work and life | Explain a consequential implication with evidence and a clear argument |
| Simon Willison | Technical builder, data tools, extensive linked writing and recurring newsletters | Publish inspectable artifacts and maintain an archive people can cite |
| The Rundown AI / Rowan Cheung | Concise recurring briefings plus practical learning products | Make the reader promise and recurring format immediately clear |
| Latent Space | AI engineering publication, interviews, research and community | Own a defined audience and go deeper than announcement summaries |

These examples support hypotheses about clear positioning, repeatable formats and useful evidence. They do not prove why a particular post went viral. Do not imitate another creator's voice, identity or proprietary material. Publisher audience totals are self-reported and were not used as performance measurements.

## What platform guidance actually supports

LinkedIn's March 2026 feed explanation emphasizes professional relevance and says it is reducing repetitive low-substance content, engagement bait and inauthentic engagement. Its September update describes further work against generic AI content. This favors a real, distinctive professional point of view; it does not establish a magic hook, posting hour or persona type.

YouTube describes personalized recommendations and performance signals. Its creator guidance makes audience retention useful for understanding where viewers disengage. Treat hook clarity, payoff and retention as measurable hypotheses; don't infer that one fixed video length is universally favored.

## Proposed format experiments

| Test | Variant A | Variant B | Primary observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Specific business consequence | Specific unanswered question | Qualified profile visits and saves at 72h |
| Evidence | Single chart with source | Small practical worksheet | Saves and report visits per 1,000 impressions |
| Short video | Show outcome first | Show problem first | Retention, completion and qualified clicks |
| Comparison | Cost per token | Cost per accepted task | Saves, substantive questions and inquiries |

Keep topic and distribution conditions as comparable as possible. Organic posts are not randomized experiments. Use repeated matched comparisons and medians, record sample sizes, and describe observed associations without claiming causality. Avoid selecting only the top posts after seeing results.

Capture analytics at fixed ages: 24h, 72h and 7d. Baseline the first two weeks before declaring winners. A large reach spike with poor reader relevance can lose to a smaller post that produces qualified subscribers or inquiries. No audience-size or revenue promise is made.

## Broader benchmarking backlog

After first-party analytics access is available, build a permission-respecting cohort of 20–30 comparable AI/business creators and code a fixed recent-post sample. Record platform, topic, format, hook class, evidence type, public engagement and post age. Public metrics cannot reveal impressions, saves or conversion unless the creator shares them. Treat those as missing, not zero. Normalize within platform and post age, and report sampling bias. Do not scrape logged-in feeds or private analytics without authorization.
